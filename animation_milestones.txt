MOVING DAY! - CHARACTER ANIMATION MILESTONES
============================================

Goal: characters that walk like they have real legs (hips swing, knees bend, feet
plant and lift) while keeping a cartoony, bouncy, plushie feel. Each animal should
still move differently from the others.

This is a plan only. Nothing here is implemented yet.


1. WHERE WE ARE TODAY
---------------------

How it works now (Player._animate_character in scripts/characters/player.gd):
- The four plushies (Bear, Bunny, Cat, Dog) are animated entirely in code. Bones
  are posed every frame by name (LLeg1, LLeg2, LArm1, ...). The Robot plays its
  own baked Idle and Walk clips and is not touched by the procedural code.
- One sine wave (_anim_time) drives everything. Legs swing +/-0.55 rad about a single
  axis. The "knee" (LLeg2/RLeg2) only tucks up to 0.4 rad on the forward swing.
- The body hops with abs(sin) and squashes and stretches. Per-character "style"
  numbers (rate, bob, roll, leg, arm, twist, nod, sync) scale the same motion.
- Idle charm is per animal (Bear breathes, Bunny twitches, Cat sways, Dog wiggles).

Why it looks wrong:
- Legs are straight sticks swinging from the hip like a pendulum or a toy soldier.
  The knee tucks in the air but does not bend on contact, so no weight is taken.
- No foot contact. Nothing decides when a foot is planted, so feet slide on the
  ground and the hop does not line up with footfalls.
- Cadence is a fixed rate (7 to 13 Hz) and not tied to ground speed, so fast and
  slow walking both look like skating.
- Only one rotation axis per joint. No hip drop, no pelvis rotation, no ankle roll.
- There are only two states, idle and walk. Starting, stopping, turning, carrying,
  lifting, shoving and throwing all reuse the same walk with arms layered on top.
- Procedural motion is tuned by feel with magic numbers, so it is hard to review
  or to compare between versions.


2. DESIGN PRINCIPLES
--------------------

- Cartoony, not realistic: exaggerate the key poses, keep timing snappy, accept a
  little squash and stretch. The knees bend like a real walk, but more.
- Contact, passing, and apex are the three poses that sell a walk. Build those, then
  blend between them.
- Speed drives everything: stride length and cadence come from real ground speed so
  feet do not slide.
- One shared walk engine, per-character personality as data (tuning tables), not
  per-character code branches.
- Never fight gameplay: animation is visual only. Collision, grab points and
  furniture carry positions must not change because a knee moved.
- Network safe: animation is derived on every machine from the velocity and state
  the game already syncs. No new network fields.
- Keep it measurable: every milestone ends with a repeatable check (frame strips
  or captured poses) so we can compare before and after.


3. MILESTONES
-------------

M0  Reference and test harness (small)
    - Build a debug scene (or a dev flag) that shows all five characters walking in
      place at slow, medium and full speed, side-on and iso camera, with a ground grid.
    - Add a headless capture script that renders a strip of N frames across one walk
      cycle per character and saves PNGs. This is how every later milestone is checked.
    - Record the current walk as the baseline strip to compare against.
    - Write down bone names, rest poses and axes for each plushie rig (the rig is
      keyed by name suffix and posed in model axes, see _build_rig and _pose_bone).
    Done when: one command produces a before/after contact sheet for any character.

M1  Real walk cycle for the plushies (core milestone)
    - Replace the single swing with a proper two-leg phase: contact, down, passing,
      up, per leg, half a cycle apart.
    - Hip (Leg1) swings forward and back; knee (Leg2) bends on the down/passing pose
      to absorb weight and straightens at contact; added ankle/foot pitch if the rig
      has foot bones, otherwise fake it with a little leg counter-rotation.
    - Body bob follows the legs: lowest at the down pose, highest at passing, not an
      abs(sin) hop detached from the feet.
    - Hip sway and pelvis yaw so the walk reads from the iso camera angle.
    - Keep exaggeration dials (knee bend, bob, squash) in the style table.
    Done when: from the iso camera and from the side, the contact sheet shows a knee
    visibly bending and straightening each step, and no leg is a rigid stick.

M2  Speed-matched stride and foot planting
    - Cadence and stride length derived from ground speed (steps per metre), clamped
      to a cartoon-friendly range. Slow walk = small slow steps, run = long quick ones.
    - Phase accumulates by distance travelled, not by time, so standing still never
      advances the cycle and a stop freezes on a valid pose.
    - Measure foot slide in the capture harness (planted foot world position over the
      stance phase) and tune until slide is below a small threshold.
    - Optional: simple two-bone IK for the planted foot if FK alone cannot hold it.
    Done when: foot slide on flat ground is under an agreed threshold at all speeds.

M3  Starts, stops and turns
    - Start step: a short lean-in and first-step anticipation instead of snapping into
      the cycle.
    - Stop: settle with a small overshoot and a brief double-support, then blend to idle.
    - Turning: lead with the head, then chest, then hips; bank into sharp turns; small
      pivot steps when changing direction while nearly stationary.
    - Reuse the existing lean/bank from acceleration, but feed it through the new poses.
    Done when: stop-start and 180 degree turns show no pop and no skating in the strips.

M4  Carrying, lifting, shoving and throwing
    - Carry: wider, lower stance with a slight lean back, shorter steps, knees bent more.
      Heavier furniture = more effort. Hands stay on the grab points.
    - Lift (hold trigger): crouch-and-raise pose, then the same carry walk.
    - Shove: a real lunge (rear leg pushes off, front knee bends) rather than just
      arms out.
    - Throw: wind-up lean back, throw forward, follow-through, recover.
    - Heave-ho (shared lift): the straining pose from the heave mechanic.
    - Two or more players carrying the same piece should visibly step in sympathy.
    Done when: each action has its own readable silhouette in the capture strips.

M5  Per-character personality as data
    - Move the existing style numbers into a richer table per animal: cadence, stride,
      knee bend, bob, hip sway, head lag, arm swing, squash, plus a "gait" flag.
    - Bear: heavy, wide, slow, big head bob.
    - Bunny: hops with both legs together, long airtime, soft landings.
    - Cat: narrow, smooth, hips lead, low bob, tail-less sway.
    - Dog: quick eager trot, bouncy, wiggly hips.
    - Keep and refine the idle charm (breathing, nose twitch, tail-wag hips, paw pat).
    Done when: with the character names hidden, each animal is identifiable by gait.

M6  Secondary motion
    - Overlap and follow-through for ears, head and any floppy parts: lag behind the
      body and settle after a stop. Simple spring or damped follow on selected bones.
    - Squash and stretch on landings and when braking hard.
    - Small anticipation and recovery on grab and drop.
    Done when: stopping dead produces a visible settle rather than a freeze.

M7  Robot parity
    - The robot uses baked clips. Decide: keep the clips and add procedural layers
      (carry arms, lean, bank, shove, throw), or retarget the new walk onto it.
    - Make its stride speed-matched like the others so it does not skate.
    - Give it the same carry, lift, shove and throw poses so it is not the odd one out.
    Done when: all five characters respond to every gameplay state.

M8  Polish, performance and safety
    - Profile with four players on screen. Animation must stay cheap (bone poses only,
      no allocations per frame in hot paths).
    - Sleep animation for characters that are far off screen (cutaway view).
    - Verify online play: remote players animate correctly from synced velocity and
      state, with no jitter on snapshot updates.
    - Re-check every gameplay mechanic that touches the model: grab positions, throw
      origin, ping marker, colour ring, sticky links, slip patches.
    - Final tuning pass with the whole team on a real controller.
    Done when: nothing regresses and a playtest with four players feels good.


4. SUGGESTED ORDER AND SIZE
---------------------------

M0 (small) -> M1 (large, the big visible win) -> M2 (medium) -> M3 (medium) ->
M4 (large) -> M5 (medium) -> M6 (small/medium) -> M7 (medium) -> M8 (small).

Ship M0 + M1 first and playtest before going further. M1 alone fixes most of the
"stiff legs" complaint. M2 and M3 remove the sliding and the pops. M4 to M7 are
where the game's actions and the characters' personality come in.


5. OPEN QUESTIONS FOR YOU
-------------------------

1. How exaggerated? "Plushie waddle" (big knee bend, big bob) or "believable with a
   wink" (realistic proportions, small exaggeration)?
2. Do the plushie rigs have foot bones? If not, we fake ankle motion by counter-
   rotating the lower leg, and IK planting (M2) is harder.
3. Robot: keep its baked clips with procedural layers on top, or rebuild it to match?
4. Should two players carrying one item step in sympathy (M4), or is that too fussy?
5. Is there a running state in the game, or is walk speed the only locomotion? Running
   would add its own gait.
6. Any hard limit on how long this takes before the next playtest?


6. RISKS
--------

- Plushie rigs are keyed by bone-name suffix and posed in model axes. A rig with
  odd bone orientation can make a naive rotation look wrong. M0 documents each rig.
- Heavy procedural posing is easy to over-tune. The capture harness and the style
  table keep changes reviewable.
- Online: remote players only see velocity and state, so any animation that needs
  extra information (for example, exact foot positions) must be derived locally.
- Collision capsule size must not change with the animation, or furniture handling
  will feel different.
